Professional bio

Numbers are only useful if someone can trust them.

I'm a graduate student in AI and machine learning at Indiana Wesleyan University, and I work with healthcare data. That work taught me early that a clean-looking report can still be wrong, and that the people who act on it are the ones who pay for the mistake. So I check where numbers come from before I build anything on top of them.

I'm also building an open-source intelligence (OSINT) tracking platform, which means I spend a lot of time asking whether a source is real, current, and actually says what someone claims it says. That same habit is what I bring to AI. New tools show up every week with demos that look finished. I want to be the person in the room who can explain, in plain words, what a tool can really do, what it needs to work, and what could go wrong.

My goal is to help healthcare and public-sector teams adopt AI carefully: not out of fear, and not out of hype, but with the right questions asked first.

Personal value proposition

I help healthcare and public-sector teams turn messy data into decisions they can trust. With hands-on experience in healthcare data and open-source intelligence, I check the data and the claims before anything gets built on them, and I explain AI in plain words so leaders know what is ready and what is still hype.
